# Deal Pivot AI: Small-Dealership Pilot Plan

**Context and scope.** I could not retrieve prior Deal Pivot AI conversations in this session; the available session-history lookup showed environment transitions, not conversation content. No product capability or Patrick decision is verified here. **Working assumption to validate:** Deal Pivot AI is intended to help dealership staff handle an in-progress deal decision or obstacle. The scenarios below are test cases, not claims about what the product can do. Before kickoff, confirm the product’s documented intended use, users, inputs, outputs, and prohibited uses; replace any out-of-scope scenario rather than stretching the product.

## Pilot setup

Run at one dealership with **3–5 participating sales staff and one sales manager**. Use the same definition of an eligible opportunity in both periods: a customer interaction that reaches a decision point within the product’s documented scope. Aim for **at least 20 eligible baseline cases and 30 pilot cases**; if volume is lower, treat results as directional and extend once, up to four weeks total.

Before collecting data, name a pilot owner and manager reviewer; document current dealership policy and the escalation path for pricing, trade appraisals, and financing; set a minimum acceptable gross/discount floor; and agree what counts as a customer-accepted next step. Use existing approved systems for customer or deal data. Keep the pilot log pseudonymous, exclude unnecessary personal and sensitive credit details, and follow dealership retention and access rules. Do not let an unverified output make commitments or replace an authorized employee’s judgment.

## Test scenarios

Use these only where they fit the confirmed product scope. Run a short practice with fictional or appropriately redacted examples before live use. For each live case, staff review any product output and decide whether to use it under normal dealership policy.

1. **Payment concern:** The customer likes the vehicle but is unsure about the proposed payment. Check whether the workflow supports a clear, accurate next step without promising unapproved pricing or financing.
2. **Trade-value gap:** The customer’s expected trade value differs from the dealership’s appraisal. Check whether the case is handled within appraisal authority and whether an appraisal review or other agreed next step is recorded.
3. **Preferred vehicle unavailable:** The requested unit is unavailable or unsuitable. Check whether any proposed alternative is actually in stock and appropriate; record whether the customer accepts a next step.
4. **Customer needs time:** The customer is interested but wants to wait, compare, or consult someone. Check for a useful, non-pressuring follow-up plan rather than an unsupported urgency claim.
5. **Financing change or uncertainty:** Terms change, or approval is uncertain. Check that the case is routed to an authorized finance employee and no approval or terms are represented as guaranteed.
6. **Control and exception cases:** Include a straightforward interaction with no apparent obstacle (to detect unnecessary intervention) and a case with missing or conflicting deal facts (to check that staff can catch the issue and verify facts rather than act on an unsupported result).

## Sequence and operating rhythm

1. **Prepare (1–2 days):** Confirm scope, safety boundaries, eligibility, metric definitions, the gross/discount floor, and the log. Brief staff on how to record a case and when to ignore or escalate an output.
2. **Baseline (5 business days):** Follow the dealership’s normal process. Log eligible cases and outcomes without changing the workflow. Record the mix of scenario types and staff so the comparison is interpretable.
3. **Practice and live pilot (10 business days):** Practice first, then use the product only within confirmed scope. For each eligible case, record whether it was used, the employee’s decision, and the outcome. A manager reviews cases daily for factual or policy issues; pause use if a serious issue appears.
4. **Review (1 day):** Compare baseline and pilot results overall and by scenario where counts allow. Ask staff what helped, what added work, and what should change. Do not treat a small pilot’s conversion difference as proof of impact.

If the dealership cannot reach the case targets in the planned period, extend once rather than lowering the denominator or counting duplicate interactions as new opportunities.

## What to record

For each case, use a pseudonymous case ID; date and scenario; staff role; product/version and whether it was used; time added; relevant facts needed to review accuracy (stored only in an approved system); output or action taken if applicable; employee rating and reason for accepting, editing, or ignoring it; manager accuracy/policy review; customer-accepted next step by end of day; sale status within seven days; and any complaint, escalation, policy issue, or material error. Record discount or gross only in a restricted dealership-approved source. Keep a daily count of eligible cases, including cases where the product was not used, so adoption and coverage have valid denominators.

## Suggested success measures and thresholds

These are **proposed pilot thresholds, not established product commitments**. Confirm or adjust them with the dealership before baseline collection. A “next step” means a specific action the customer accepts or agrees to, such as a follow-up appointment, test drive, appraisal review, or verified alternative—not merely a logged staff action.

| Measure | Suggested threshold | How to judge it |
|---|---|---|
| Logging coverage | At least 80% of eligible cases recorded in each period | Compare the log with the manager’s daily eligible-case tally. |
| Staff use | Product used in at least 70% of eligible pilot cases within confirmed scope | Report reasons for non-use; do not count out-of-scope cases against adoption. |
| Primary outcome | Same-day customer-accepted next-step rate improves by at least 10 percentage points over baseline | Compare similar scenario mixes; report counts and percentage-point change, not just percentages. Treat as directional at these sample sizes. |
| Output quality, if the product produces an output | At least 90% of reviewed outputs are materially accurate and within scope; **zero severe** factual, policy, or customer-harm events | Manager reviews all pilot cases if feasible, otherwise all exceptions plus a consistent sample. Any severe issue triggers a pause and review. If the product has a different core output, define an equivalent quality check before kickoff. |
| Staff burden | Median added time no more than 2 minutes per eligible case | Record time for all cases where practical; also note unusually long cases. |
| Business and customer guardrails | No breach of the pre-agreed gross/discount floor; no substantiated complaint or privacy/policy incident attributable to use | Review with the manager; do not trade customer trust or policy compliance for more next steps. |

Track sales within seven days as a secondary descriptive measure, not the sole success test. A sale can depend on factors outside the product’s influence.

## Decision after the pilot

- **Advance to a larger monitored cohort** only if the minimum case counts are met, no guardrail is breached, logging coverage and staff use meet their suggested thresholds, output quality passes where applicable, and the primary outcome meets its suggested improvement threshold without unacceptable added time.
- **Tune and extend once** if the case count is too small or results are promising but inconclusive, provided there is no severe safety, accuracy, privacy, or policy issue. Change one clearly documented workflow or training item, then remeasure.
- **Pause and redesign or stop** after any severe attributable incident; repeated material errors; a breached business floor; or, with adequate use and sample, no meaningful improvement in the primary outcome or unacceptable staff burden. Do not expand solely because staff like the tool or because a few deals closed.

**Open decisions before kickoff:** confirm the actual product workflow and its intended users; what data may be entered and retained; who may approve or override outputs; the dealership’s primary business goal and acceptable margin floor; which cases are in scope; and whether the dealership can provide a trustworthy baseline count.
